Denis Werth

Theoretical Physicist & Cosmologist
exploring the fundamental laws of Nature and the early Universe

Learn More

I am currently a postdoctoral research fellow at the Max Planck Institute for Physics in Munich, Germany, in the Quantum Field Theory group. I am also part of the UNIVERSE+ collaboration that aims to create a new mathematical language to describe phenomena on all scales, from the interactions of elementary particles to the large-scale structure of the Universe. I am funded by the Max Planck-IAS-NTU Center for Particle Physics, Cosmology and Geometry.

Previously, I was a doctoral student at the Institut d'Astrophysique de Paris and Sorbonne University, in France.

My research interests lie at the interface between theoretical high-energy physics and primordial cosmology. I see the early Universe as the ultimate laboratory where high-energy theories can be tested on the most fundamental scales. Using modern quantum field theory approaches, I develop new methods to deepen our understanding of the Universe's origins. I am particularly interested in using cosmological observations to search for evidence of new physics.

Recent News

  • October 2026: A beautiful new tool for massive cosmological correlators!

    • Jonathan Gräfe has built an online tool where you draw any tree graph, pick a root, and directly read off the series for the corresponding massive correlator, ready to copy as LaTeX. It is based on the spectral gluing algorithm of arXiv:2607.18223, and it can even assemble the full correlator with all on-shell terms and every Schwinger–Keldysh component. Go and play with it: changing the root or the energy ordering gives you the expansion in a different kinematic region.

  • October 2026: Back from the UNIVERSE+ annual retreat in Cambridge!

    • A very intense week, but incredible to reconnect with colleagues and friends from Taipei, Amsterdam, Leipzig and Cambridge. Many fruitful discussions, lots of new crazy ideas on the table, and quite a few projects waiting to be started. Now the hard part: finding the time to do them all!

  • September 2026: New paper "Carving out the Multifield Cosmological Collider Landscape" out on the ArXiv!

    • A fun short project that started off as an AI experiment and ended up somewhere I did not expect. It turns out that when inflation involves a large number of fields, almost-local cosmological collider signals in the squeezed bispectrum are no longer fine-tuned but become the generic outcome.

  • August 2026: Had a wonderful week at COSMO-26 in Leiden!

    • Leiden turned out to be a beautiful mini-Amsterdam, steeped in physics history. Hard not to think of Ehrenfest, Kramers, Lorentz, de Sitter, and the rest of the old Leiden school walking those very streets. Talks throughout the week were excellent, discussions were plentiful, and best of all, one of them turned into a brand new project with colleagues from Cambridge!

  • August 2026: New paper "Analytic Continuation of Conformal Integrals in Momentum Space" out on the ArXiv!

    • Another very nice collaboration with Jonathan and Prashanth, this time digging into the analytic structure of triple-K and multiple-K integrals that solve the conformal Ward identities in momentum space. We built new series representations that actually converge throughout the physical kinematic region, something existing expansions failed to do.

  • July 2026: New paper "All Tree-Level Massive Cosmological Correlators via Spectral Gluing" out on the ArXiv!

    • Incredible working with Jonathan on this one, very pleasant, and quite proud of what we found. We dove into generalised hypergeometric functions of the Lauricella kind, which was a lot of fun, and we uncovered some genuinely beautiful combinatorics hiding behind seemingly terrifying expressions.

  • June 2026: Had a really nice week visiting the UK, with talks at the University of Cambridge and Imperial College London!

    • Always a pleasure to catch up with colleagues, get fresh eyes on ongoing projects, and discover new corners of the UK cosmology community. Thanks to everyone who made the trip so enjoyable!
